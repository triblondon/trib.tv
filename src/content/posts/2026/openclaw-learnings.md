I heard about OpenClaw in one of those fragments of conversation you get when you are on an escalator and pass people going the other way, and you get an out of context 3 seconds that captures your attention but is instantly gone, like a sudden cliffhanger that is never resolved.  In my case they said something like "... about ClaudeBot is I think it hates me".

OK, what.  A few seconds of searching later I'm reading about this thing, and watching a bunch of YouTube videos.  I concluded fairly quickly that the primary use cases for (what is at time of writing called) OpenClaw are:

* Vacuous content marketeers / influencers generating slop content
* Trader bros monitoring markets / executing trades
* Vibe coders trying to pretend they have an actual team of proficient software engineers working for them

I hate all these people. And almost everything you can read about OpenClaw online is itself just slop generated by AI.

But you know, this OpenClaw thing is actually a great platform to demonstrate what AI is good at, and what it's bad at.  And maybe to actually generate some cool automations.  I started with something very easy and something very hard:

- Add something to my calendar
- Be a constant, omniscient companion that serves up nuggets of information, unprompted, exactly when needed 

### Doing easy things badly and at huge cost

It didn't start well.

Mentioning my interest to a friend, they excitedly demoed to me that they could message their bot ("Husky", for some reason) on Telegram and it would add an event to their calendar.  The prompt was a fairly long sentence and the response time for confirmation from the bot was ~10 seconds.  I could do it faster and with less cognitive load by just opening the bloody calendar app and adding the event myself.  Besides which, why replace deterministic reminders that are guaranteed to fire at exactly the right time and are free, with something that might or might not work and is very expensive?

This led me to my first realisation: OpenClaw is useful only when the use case is nuanced.  If it's going to do reminders or calendar events, maybe ...

### Failing at hard things

In later Mission Impossible films, Simon Pegg's Benji Dunn often yells at Ethan Hunt over a radio link telling him where to turn and exactly what to do, and inexplicably knows not only exactly where Hunt is but also where all the bad guys are, the exact layout of whatever building Hunt is currently destroying, and everything that's going on anywhere else that might be about to affect the mission.

Now, Openclaw could know where I am and could use Google Maps APIs to do routing and path finding, and could know what my plans are via calendar, so is it possible it could send me targeted messages to tell me things that might help me out in that exact moment?  Like if you know I'm heading for my parents' house and that I normally take the train, and it looks like i'm heading for the train station, check upcoming departures to make sure the trains are running.  What I want is:

> "Andrew, the Windrush line is suspended today due to engineering works.  If you're heading for your parents' right now, I suggest you pivot to a 388 bus and transfer to the tube at London Bridge"

The reality was a bit different.  It occasionally said useful things.  But mostly, it would send me a constant stream of instructions to please get on the train that I'm already on.

The problem with this idea is it is actually a hard problem and requires *too much context*, much of which has to be *guessed or extrapolated*.
